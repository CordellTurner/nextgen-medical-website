02 / LISTED PEPTIDE OPTIONS

What the evidence says.

CJC-1295

An investigational growth-hormone-releasing-hormone analog. A small study in healthy adults measured changes in growth hormone and IGF-1; it did not establish a clinical wellness benefit. FDA notes potential immune reactions and peptide impurity concerns, with reported increases in heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reactions for compounded CJC-1295.

Human study FDA compounding safety

BPC-157

An investigational peptide. A 2025 systematic review found that the available research was predominantly preclinical, with very limited clinical evidence and insufficient human safety data to establish therapeutic benefit.

2025 systematic review FDA compounding safety

Ipamorelin

An investigational growth-hormone secretagogue with no FDA-approved therapeutic indication identified. FDA cites serious adverse events in an intravenous clinical study and notes potential immune reactions, impurities, and limited safety information for other routes.

Clinical study FDA compounding safety

PT-141 (bremelanotide)

The branded prescription product Vyleesi is FDA-approved for acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women. That approval is limited to the product and indication in its label; it does not establish approval for other populations or uses.

Vyleesi prescribing information

GHK-Cu

A copper-binding peptide studied in experimental settings. Injectable GHK-Cu is not an FDA-approved treatment for a medical condition. FDA notes that injectable compounded preparations may pose concerns including immune reactions, aggregation, impurities, and limited human safety information.

FDA compounding safety

MOTS-c

A mitochondria-derived peptide studied in experimental research. No FDA-approved drug product or therapeutic use was identified. FDA reports it has not identified human drug-exposure data for proposed compounded products and notes potential immune reaction and impurity concerns.

FDA compounding safety

Tesamorelin

FDA-approved tesamorelin products have a specific indication to reduce excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy. That product-specific approval is not a general weight-management indication.

Egrifta SV prescribing information Egrifta WR prescribing information

TB-500 (thymosin beta-4 fragment)

TB-500 is a peptide fragment. No FDA-approved drug product or therapeutic indication was identified. FDA reports no identified human drug-exposure data for products containing the fragment and notes potential immune-reaction, aggregation, and impurity concerns for compounding.

FDA compounding safety

Epitalon (Epithalon)

An investigational peptide without an FDA-approved product or established anti-aging indication. FDA notes it has not identified safety information for proposed routes of administration and flags potential immune-reaction and impurity concerns for compounded products.

FDA compounding safety

Sermorelin

Sermorelin is a growth-hormone-releasing-hormone analog. The historical FDA-approved Geref product had pediatric diagnostic and treatment uses; that product history does not establish approval for adult anti-aging, weight loss, or body-composition uses.

FDA Drugs@FDA record

GLP-1 therapies

Semaglutide and tirzepatide are distinct medicines with product-specific FDA labels. Wegovy (semaglutide) and Zepbound (tirzepatide) have labeled weight-management uses for eligible patients. Other products containing the same ingredients have different approved indications. FDA has reported dosing errors with compounded injectable semaglutide and notes that semaglutide salt forms are different active ingredients from those in approved medicines.

Wegovy prescribing information Zepbound prescribing information FDA information on unapproved GLP-1 drugs

FDA identifies potential compounding safety concerns for certain bulk substances; this does not mean every possible product or use has the same status. See FDA's substance-specific safety information. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality before marketing; compounding is subject to separate legal conditions.

03 / WHO IT MAY BE FOR

Discuss your questions with a provider.

If you are considering peptide therapy, share your health history, concerns, medications, and goals. Your provider can discuss current service availability and whether further evaluation is appropriate. 04 / WHAT TO EXPECT

Get clear answers before deciding.

Ask which specific product is being considered, whether it is FDA-approved for the proposed use or compounded, what evidence supports that specific use, known risks, alternatives, monitoring, and total cost. If a service is appropriate and available, your provider can explain the process and follow-up.
